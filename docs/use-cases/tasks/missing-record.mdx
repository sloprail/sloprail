## The failure

A situation that should have produced a record — a decision made, a memory worth
keeping — produces nothing. Nobody notices the absence, because there is nothing
there to notice. The record that should exist and the record that was never made
look identical from the outside: empty. The cost lands later, when the reasoning
behind a choice is needed and was never written down.

<UseCase id="missing-record" label="Watch it happen — then get refused" />

## How it works

The trigger and the record are tied together: when a situation calls for a record,
the turn must declare it and produce the artifact. Two gates enforce the two
halves — that a record was declared at all, and that the declared record actually
landed.

<Steps>

1. A **context** wakes on the declaring tag or a touch under the record's paths,
   and accumulates what showed up, so the gates can read it back at `Stop`.

2. A **structure** file-guard holds the record files to their allowed layout, so
   a record can only land where it belongs.

3. Two **gates** at `Stop`: one refuses when the situation called for a record and
   none was declared; the other refuses when a record was declared but the
   artifact never landed.

</Steps>

A context can only track; the gates are what refuse the turn.
