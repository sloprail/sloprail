# A rule is the shortest text that still carries its meaning

**Mistake:** padding a rule until it reads like documentation — restating the
title as a sentence, explaining that the mistake is bad, listing what the rule is
not, closing with a summary of what was just said.

A rule is read at authoring time by someone deciding whether their hook has the
shape it warns about. Every line that does not help that decision costs the
reader attention and buys nothing, and a rule nobody finishes reading enforces
nothing.

**Instead:** name the mistake, show it, say what to do instead, and stop. Where
the rule was measured rather than reasoned, the measurement is the shortest thing
that earns belief — keep it. Where a sentence could be deleted without changing
what an author does differently, delete it.

Concretely, noise is: a preamble before the mistake; a restatement of the
heading; "it is important to note"; a benefit already implied by the fix; a
closing recap; a second example making the first one's point again.

Length is not the test — a long rule carrying dense, non-obvious content is
high-signal, and a three-line rule padded with a recap is not. The test is
whether any span can be cut without losing something the reader acts on.
