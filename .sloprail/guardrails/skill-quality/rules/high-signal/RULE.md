# A skill is the shortest text that still carries its meaning

**Mistake:** padding a skill until it reads like a manual — an introduction
saying what the skill will cover, a restatement of the heading as a sentence,
"it is important to note", a benefit already implied by the instruction, a
closing recap of what was just read.

A skill is loaded into a context window ahead of the work and read by an agent
deciding what to do next. Every line that does not change what the agent does
costs the budget the actual task needs, and the padding is what gets skimmed
past — so the one load-bearing sentence is read at the same depth as the recap
around it.

**Instead:** say the thing, show it, and stop. Where a claim was measured rather
than reasoned, the measurement is the shortest thing that earns belief — keep
it. Where a sentence could be deleted without changing what the reader does,
delete it.

Length is not the test. A long section carrying dense, non-obvious procedure is
high-signal; a five-line section wrapped in a preamble and a summary is not. The
test is whether any span can be cut without losing something the reader acts on.

**Reasoning that changes a decision is not padding.** A passage saying why the
work is done this way, what failure it avoids, or what counts as finished sets
the standard the reader then applies — cut it and the instructions are followed
to the letter and missed in substance. Padding restates; rationale decides.
Flag a passage only when you can say what the reader would do identically
without it.
